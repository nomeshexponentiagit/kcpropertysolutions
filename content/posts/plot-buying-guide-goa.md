Buying land in Goa can be one of the best investment decisions you make — but only if you do it right. Goa's unique land laws and rapid development mean buyers need to be extra cautious. Here are the five most important checks before you sign that deal.

## 1. Verify the Title Deed

The title deed is the most critical document. It proves the seller's ownership and the chain of previous owners. Get a lawyer to trace the title back at least 30 years. Any break in the chain is a red flag.

## 2. Check the Survey Number

Every plot in Goa has a unique survey number. Cross-check this with the local Village Panchayat and the Directorate of Settlement and Land Records. Ensure the plot boundaries match what the seller claims.

## 3. Understand the Zone

Goa's Regional Plan 2021 (and upcoming 2031) classifies land into zones — settlement, orchard, paddy field, forest, etc. Only settlement-zoned land can be used for residential construction. Converting non-settlement land is a long and uncertain process.

## 4. Confirm Road Access

A plot without proper road access is practically useless. Verify that the access road is public and recorded in government records. Private roads through someone else's property can lead to disputes later.

## 5. Check for Encumbrances

Get an Encumbrance Certificate (EC) from the Sub-Registrar's office. This shows any mortgages, liens, or legal claims on the property. An EC going back 15-30 years with no entries is what you want to see.

## The Bottom Line

With Goa's real estate market growing at an unprecedented pace, the right plot in the right location can multiply your investment.
